Ask anyone who runs a digital asset management system (a DAM) where the time goes, and the answer is usually the same: describing things. Every image, video and document needs a title, a description, keywords and a dozen other details before anyone can find it again. That information is called metadata, and most of it is still typed in by a person, one asset at a time.

That is the gap AI agents fill. An agent can suggest the metadata, or run a check using a model trained for one specific job, and save a team a lot of typing. [Orange Logic's AI features](https://www.orangelogic.com/dam-platform/artificial-intelligence) include agents that enrich metadata as content arrives.

## What an agent is

In Orange Logic, an agent is given its own purpose and instructions and is configured to run inside the [workflow automations](https://www.orangelogic.com/dam-platform/workflows) a team already has. Orange Logic's tool for building them is called [Agent Studio](https://www.orangelogic.com/products/digital-asset-management-agent-studio). Orange Logic also lets a team [connect outside AI services and build its own integrations](https://www.orangelogic.com/products/digital-asset-management-integrations), and a custom integration of that kind behaves much the same way as an agent built in Agent Studio. Either way, the agent is one step in a process, not a separate tool people have to remember to open.

## The person stays in charge

The part we care most about is what happens to the agent's answer.

What the AI writes can go into its own dedicated field, clearly marked as coming from AI. Nothing is hidden and nothing is mistaken for a person's work. When someone approves it, that approval can copy the text into the standard field, such as the Description everyone sees, or into other fields that hold governed data.

How people approve is up to the team. It can be a custom button, a workflow status, or one of many other options, including tagging a whole batch at once. The agent proposes. A person decides.

## Where agents help, and where they don't

Not everything needs AI. When a task follows clear logic, a plain automation is more consistent, and consistency is what you want from a system of record. Agents are worth considering for the larger, messier jobs, where the input varies too much for a rule to handle.

So our first question on any project is not "where can we add AI?" It is "what is this team doing by hand, and what kind of problem is it?"
